# Assignment #1: Article Critique

The article I chose for this critique is “Knowledge, attitudes, practices, and barriers of artificial intelligence as predictors of intent to stay among nurses: A cross-sectional study.” In my opinion, the title is clear regarding the variables that the researchers wanted to study, but it does not mention the country or region where the study was conducted. The abstract includes all the main aspects of the research, including the objectives, methodology, results, and conclusion.

In the introduction, the authors clearly explained the research problem. Based on two previous studies, nurses are concerned about losing their jobs or being undervalued in the future. This makes the study important in Jordan, especially to understand nurses' knowledge of AI and the barriers they face when using it. The authors did not directly mention any hypotheses or research questions. However, we can understand them from the aim of the study: “Therefore, this study aimed to describe the level of knowledge, attitudes, practices, and perceived barriers of AI among nurses in Jordan and their influence on nurses intent to stay in their job positions in Jordanian hospitals.” The authors supported these ideas with recent studies.

There was no separate section for the literature review. Instead, the authors included previous research in the introduction to support their ideas and the aim of the study. All the references were published within the last five years, except for one study from 2014. One of the studies was conducted in Syria, which is a neighboring country with a similar culture to Jordan. I think this makes it a good reference for the study. Including studies from China also allows the reader to understand the topic from a wider perspective.

The authors did not clearly define the main concepts of the study, such as artificial intelligence, knowledge, attitudes, practices, perceived barriers, and intent to stay. They provided some examples and explained the context, but the actual definitions were missing. There was also no conceptual or theoretical framework, and the authors did not explain why they did not include one. Although they discussed the relationship between these variables and nurse retention based on previous research, I think using an established theoretical model would have helped explain these relationships better.

Regarding ethical considerations, the researchers obtained approval from the hospital administrations and explained the study to the participants before collecting their signed informed consent. The nurses completed the questionnaires in a quiet room, which provided more privacy and reduced distractions. The researchers also obtained approval from the institutional review board (IRB) at Zarqa University.

The researchers used a descriptive correlational cross-sectional design, which I think was suitable for the aim of the study since they wanted to examine the relationships between different variables at one point in time. However, one concern I had was that the questionnaires included the participants' names. This might affect their answers because some nurses may respond according to what they believe is socially acceptable rather than expressing their actual opinions.

The target population included nurses across 50 Jordanian hospitals, while the accessible population included nurses from four governmental hospitals. The inclusion and exclusion criteria were clearly mentioned. However, the researchers used nonprobability convenience sampling, which increases the possibility of selection bias. The authors acknowledged this limitation and suggested using probability-based sampling in future studies to improve representation. One of the strengths of the study was that the sample size of 148 participants was calculated using G*Power, with an additional 25% to account for nonresponses.

Regarding data collection, although the authors did not provide clear conceptual definitions of the variables, they explained how these variables were measured using a Likert scale questionnaire. In my opinion, depending only on self-reported questionnaires might not accurately reflect how nurses actually use AI in their clinical practice. Direct observation could have provided additional information.

The questionnaire showed good validity, with CVI values of 0.91 and 0.93. The reliability was also high, with Cronbach’s alpha values ranging from 0.85 to 0.92 across the subscales. However, the pilot study included only 10 nurses. I think including a larger number of participants, possibly 20 or more, could have helped the researchers evaluate the questionnaire more thoroughly.

For data analysis, the researchers used SPSS 26 to perform descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and a multivariate linear regression model. The VIF values were below 10, and the Durbin-Watson statistic was 1.89. The statistical methods were suitable for examining whether knowledge, attitudes, practices, and barriers related to AI could predict nurses' intent to stay in their jobs. The results were presented in six tables that included statistical significance values. The tables provided useful information for future research, although the exact confidence intervals were not reported.

In the discussion, the authors compared their findings with previous studies conducted in Syria, Egypt, China, and Saudi Arabia. They were also careful when explaining the relationships between the variables without claiming direct causation, which is appropriate considering the cross-sectional design.

The authors mentioned the limitations of their study, including convenience sampling, self-reported responses, and limited representation of the population. They also provided recommendations related to nursing education, hospital administration, and health policy.

Overall, I think the study was well organized and used appropriate statistical methods to answer its main objective. Although there were some weaknesses in the theoretical framework, sampling method, and data collection, the study provides useful information about the relationship between AI and nurses' willingness to stay in their jobs in Jordan.
