The abstract mentioned all the aspects of the research, objectives, methodology, results and conclusion.

While the author stated the problem in the introduction he was clear that the nurses are afraid of losing their jobs or being undervalued in future according to two previous studies, that makes the study valuable and important in his country, to study the knowledge about AI and the barriers of using it. The author did not mention the any hypothesis, research questions in a direct manner, but from the context we can guess both of them when he mentioned “Therefore, this study aimed to describe the level of knowledge, attitudes, practices, and perceived barriers of AI among nurses in Jordan and their influence on nurses intent to stay in their job positions in Jordanian hospitals.” all of these ideas are supported by recent prior research.

The key concepts in the study, such as artificial intelligence, knowledge, attitudes, practices, perceived barriers, and intent to stay, are not adequately defined conceptually, the authors treat them as selfevident and only describe the context and examples rather than their meanings. also the text does not include a conceptual or theoretical framework, rationale, or map, without justifying this absence. Although the authors briefly mention a dynamic interplay between these variables and nurse retention based on previous literature, they did not use an established model, which weakens the theoretical foundation needed to explain how these factors influence nurses intent to stay.

To safeguard participant rights, the researchers obtained hospital administrative approvals, explained the study clearly, collected signed informed consent, and allowed nurses to complete the questionnaires in a quiet room to ensure privacy, which helped minimize risks for bias and maximize comfort. The author mentioned he had approval from an institutional review board (IRB) from Zarqa University. Regarding the research design, a descriptive correlational cross sectional design was used, which is appropriate for the study aim of examining relationships at a single point in time. Collecting data at one time minimized bias, and using a quiet room reduced distractions, a self reported questionnaire with showing your name allows participants to match their answers to meet perceived social expectations or demands.

The target population of nurses across 50 Jordanian hospitals and the accessible population from four governmental hospitals were clearly identified, with specific inclusion and exclusion criteria, though the use of nonprobability convenience sampling increases selection bias, in the limitation part he mentioned that point with possible changing the method in future research to probability based sampling techniques in enhance representation. the positive side that the sample size of 148 participants was justified through a G*Power analysis with a 25% buffer for nonresponses, ensuring adequate statistical power. For data collection and measurement, while formal conceptual definitions were missing earlier, the variables were clearly operationalized using a well described likert scale questionnaire, even though relying purely on self reports may not capture actual clinical AI practices as accurately as direct observation, but the authors provided strong evidence of tool quality but on small pilot sample size, the validity showed high numbers (CVI of 0.91 and 0.93), pilot testing with 10 nurses was less than the minimum number the author could do it at least  for more 20 nurses to get higher validity, and high internal reliability across all subscales (Cronbach’s alpha ranging from 0.85 to 0.92).

The data analysis was well aligned with the study objective, utilizing SPSS 26 to perform descriptive statistics, pearson correlation, and a multivariate linear regression model that effectively controlled for confounding variables, with VIF values below 10 and a Durbin-Watson statistic of 1.89. These appropriate statistical methods directly evaluated the predictive relationships between AI knowledge, attitudes, practices, and barriers on nurses intent to stay. The findings were clearly summarized across six detailed tables, presenting statistical significance values, providing sufficient quantitative detail to facilitate future meta-analyses and evidence-based practice even though exact confidence intervals were not explicitly listed.

The discussion interprets the results within the context of prior international research from countries like Syria, Egypt, China, and Saudi Arabia, maintaining appropriate caution by not overstating causal relationships given the cross-sectional design. The authors transparently address study limitations such as convenience sampling, self-reported response bias, and restricted representativeness while offering practical, actionable recommendations for nursing education, hospital administration, and health policy, the study demonstrates strong internal validity and clear organization, providing meaningful insights into how AI integration affects nurse retention in Jordan and making a valuable contribution to the nursing discipline.
